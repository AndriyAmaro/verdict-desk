# Rules v2 diagnosis: market regime or built-in bias?

**Status: measurement only.** This study changed no rule, no threshold, no workflow and no ledger line. What to do with the result belongs to the owner of the project.

## The question

From dossier 54 on, the ledger records dossiers with the v2 rules (`verdict_desk/agents_v2.py`), and the hourly workflow runs `decide-v2`. The owner counted 17 LONG, 3 FLAT and 0 SHORT in the first 20 v2 dossiers and asked whether that mix is the market or a bias in the thresholds. At dossier 77, the head when this was written, the count is 18 LONG, 3 FLAT and 0 SHORT in 21 dossiers.

## The answer

**1. The live mix is the market regime.** All 21 v2 dossiers had EMA12 above EMA48 (BTCUSDT 9, ETHUSDT 6, SOLUSDT 6). In 18 of them the MACD histogram was positive too (`both bullish` in the tables) and all 18 decided LONG; the other 3 had mixed signals and decided FLAT. None was recorded in a `both bearish` state, so the live chain has not tested the SHORT side yet. In the replay, a bullish state decides LONG 72 to 75% of the time and the mirror state decides SHORT 93 to 96% of the time, so the rules would have printed SHORT on a falling tape. That 18 of 18 sits above the 72 to 75% of the history is no surprise: consecutive hourly dossiers of three symbols recorded inside one uptrend are far from independent draws.

**2. The thresholds are not LONG-biased. They lean SHORT.** Replayed over 3,928 hourly windows per symbol, and again over the mirrored history (every move negated), v2 decides SHORT about twice as often as LONG whichever way the market goes. The bias, meaning the part of LONG minus SHORT that does not flip under the mirror, is -18.4 pp on BTCUSDT, -19.2 on ETHUSDT and -19.9 on SOLUSDT (-18.1, -18.0 and -19.2 with a second mirror that inverts prices). The v1 rules, run through the same test as a control, sit at -0.1 pp on all three. On the real history v2 decides about 20% LONG, 38% SHORT and 41% FLAT; v1 decides 5%, 5% and 90%.

**3. Where the lean shows.** With the same two core signals on each side (EMA trend and MACD sign), the bullish state ends in LONG 72 to 75% of the time and the bearish state ends in SHORT 93 to 96% of the time. When the two signals disagree (`mixed`), v2 still says SHORT in 27 to 31% of the windows and LONG in about 3%. The section "What differs between the two sides" lists the causes found in `agents_v2.py`.

**4. Context from the backtest protocol.** On the same history v2 trades 1.6 to 1.8 times as often as v1 and loses more on all three symbols: net -55.22%, -88.38% and -11.36% against -26.86%, -15.16% and -0.78%, with the same fee of 0.05% per side. Both of v2's sides lose money on every symbol, so the loss cannot be assigned to the direction mix alone. This is past data under a simplified protocol, not live performance.

## What differs between the two sides in `agents_v2.py`

Read from the file, not measured. Both sides share the v1 arguments (trend, 24-candle range break, 24-candle momentum, volume with direction, funding, news). The v2 additions, and the weights the judge adds up, are not mirror images of each other:

| | Bull side (11 argument types) | Bear side (16 argument types) |
|---|---|---|
| RSI | below 35: 1.25 | above 65: 1.0, and above 75 adds 1.25 |
| Bollinger %B | below 0.1: 1.0; close below the lower band: 1.25 | above 0.85: 1.0, and above 0.95 adds 1.25; close above the upper band: 1.25 |
| MACD histogram | positive: 1.0 | negative: 1.0; price up while the histogram is negative: 1.5 |
| 24-candle momentum | above +2%: 0.75 | below -2%: 1.0, and below -4% adds 1.5 |
| Trend | EMA12 above EMA48: 1.5 | EMA12 below EMA48: 1.5; price below both EMAs: 1.0 |
| 24-candle range | close above the high: 2.0 | close below the low: 2.0; close within 0.2% of the low: 0.75 |
| Volume | rising price on volume above 1.5x: 0.75; volume above 2x, whatever the price does: 1.0 | falling price on volume above 1.5x: 0.75 |

Three things stand out. Six bear arguments have no bull counterpart (price below both EMAs, close near the 24-candle low, the bearish divergence, the severe-drop step, RSI above 75 and %B above 0.95), and in the decision counts above "price below both EMAs" alone would flip 17 to 22% of the SHORT decisions on each symbol. The shared thresholds are not mirrored: the bear momentum weight is 1.0 against 0.75, and the Bollinger trigger is 0.85 on the bear side against 0.1 on the bull side (its mirror would be 0.15). One argument leans the other way: "volume surge" adds 1.0 to the bull side even when the price is falling. The measured net effect is still the SHORT lean in the tables.

History of those rules, from `git log` of the two v2 files: three commits, all on 2026-10-20 (`4b19f54` introduced v2, `61e886d` lowered the entry threshold from 2.0 to 1.5 and made the workflow run only v2, `a836c6a` "v2 bear signals stronger" raised bear weights and added bear arguments so that SHORT could fire). Their messages cite more decisive output as the goal, and the last one cites a simulated bearish case as its check. None of the three reports a mirror test or a backtest of v2.

## What this study does not show

- It uses price and volume only. Funding and news do not exist in the history. For the 21 live dossiers, recomputing the verdict without them changes 0 of 21, so the replay is a fair stand-in for them, but that says nothing about every future hour. The funding arguments, inherited from v1, are not mirrored either (the bull side fires below 0, the bear side above 0.0005), and this study cannot measure that part.
- The history is 3,999 closed hourly candles per symbol (about 5.5 months, 2026-05-07 to 2026-10-21) from the public Binance spot mirror: one history, three correlated symbols, each of which ended higher than it started.
- The mirror test takes a market that rises and falls symmetrically as the fair reference. The two mirrors (negated arithmetic returns, inverted prices) agree within 1.2 pp, and the v1 control comes out at -0.1 pp or better on both.
- The 21 live dossiers are too few, and too correlated, to say anything about direction beyond the regime they were recorded in.
- The walk-forward figures are past data under the same simplified protocol as the published v1 backtest (entry at the close, one position at a time, 48-candle time exit, 0.05% fee per side, no slippage). They are not live performance, and this project makes no claim that its rules are profitable: the claim is that the decisions are recorded in a way anyone can audit and replay.
- `python3 -m verdict_desk replay` re-derived all 77 dossiers with matching verdicts when this was written, and `verify` found the chain intact, so the study left the integrity of the record alone.

## Decisions that belong to the owner

Nothing below has been done.

1. **Keep v2 as it is.** The asymmetry stays and is documented here. In a flat or falling market v2 then records about twice as many SHORT as LONG dossiers.
2. **Return the hourly workflow to the v1 rules** (`decide` instead of `decide-v2` in `.github/workflows/hourly.yml`), **or record both** (the workflow had a dual-record step until `61e886d`). v1 is symmetric by this test (-0.1 pp) and is the rule set of the published backtest, but it answers FLAT about 90% of the time. Recording both doubles the dossiers per run.
3. **Remove the asymmetry in a new rule set.** A change to the v2 weights has to ship as a new `rules_version`, not as an edit of `agents_v2.py`: `replay` re-derives each dossier with the current code of the version recorded in it, so editing v2 in place would make the v2 dossiers already in the ledger fail to replay. This module is the acceptance test to run first: the bias should be near 0 pp on both mirrors for all three symbols, and only then are the walk-forward numbers worth reading.

## Reproduce

```bash
python3 -m verdict_desk.v2_diagnosis --until 2026-10-21T07:00:00Z --candles 3999 --out research/V2_DIAGNOSIS.md
python3 -m unittest tests.test_v2_diagnosis
```

The first command fetches the same closed candles (the fingerprints in the first line of the tables must match), reads `ledger/ledger.jsonl` after verifying its chain, and rewrites only the part between the markers. Without `--until` it measures the latest 4,000 candles instead. The module is `verdict_desk/v2_diagnosis.py`; it only reads the ledger and never appends to it.
