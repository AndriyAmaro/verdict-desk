Evidence — what the record can and cannot do for a lawyer

Not legal advice. Whether a record is admitted, and how much weight it carries, is for the court and depends on the jurisdiction. This page only states, layer by layer, what the technology establishes and what it leaves open, so that a lawyer can decide what to ask a qualified person to certify. Counsel should review any declaration before it is signed.

Layer by layer

LayerWhat it establishesHow a lawyer might use itWhat it does not establish
Frozen evidence packet
source, time, hash per item		 What the agent was shown at that moment, and from where. A source that failed is recorded as a gap, never filled in. Chain of custody for the inputs: the exhibit places every claim beside the exact evidence text it cites. That the source was telling the truth. A literal headline can be wrong.
Hash chain
RFC 8785 canonical JSON, SHA-256, each record commits to the previous one		 That a record was not changed after it was written, and its place in the order. Editing one flags that record; re-hashing it breaks the next link. Authentication of an electronic record: evidence that the item is what its proponent says it is (in US federal practice, the standard of Rule 901(a)). Who wrote it, or that the operator did not rewrite the entire chain. That is what the layers below are for.
Replay
python3 -m verdict_desk replay		 That the recorded verdict is what the stated rules give from the frozen evidence: the process produces the same result when run again. Support for a description of the process as one that "produces an accurate result" (the language of Rule 902(13)). The qualified person must describe and vouch for the process. That the rules are wise, or that the decision was right. The analysts' text is frozen, not regenerated.
Hash-verified copy
the exhibit recomputes its hash when rendered		 That a copy handed to you is identical, bit for bit, to the record in the chain. The identification-by-hash that Rule 902(14) describes for data copied from a file or device, together with a certification by a qualified person. That nobody certified it: the rule requires a certification, and notice to the opponent. The generator below drafts one.
Bitcoin, through OpenTimestamps
every hour; checked in your browser		 That the head of the chain, and so every earlier record it commits to, existed no later than the block that contains the stamp. Neither we nor a block explorer can change that. Strong supporting evidence of the time by which the record existed, and a defence against "you rewrote it later". It is not a qualified electronic time stamp under eIDAS. Article 41(2) gives its presumption of accuracy of date, time and integrity only to qualified time stamps issued by a qualified trust service provider; Article 41(1) says other electronic time stamps cannot be denied effect solely because they are electronic or not qualified, which leaves their weight to the court.
Ethereum Sepolia anchor
through a Chainlink CRE workflow		 A second, independent record of a head, in a contract that only accepts increasing sequence numbers. Redundancy for the Bitcoin stamp. A testnet is not a durable public record, and the anchor is not hourly. Treat it as a demonstration of the mechanism.
Git history Commit times held by a third-party host, and copies in every earlier clone. A rewrite would show against any earlier clone. The commit times are those of the operator's own account: the weakest witness.

Rules referred to: US Federal Rules of Evidence, Rule 902(13) and (14) (certified records generated by an electronic process; certified data copied from a file, identified by hash); Regulation (EU) No 910/2014, Article 41 (electronic time stamps); RFC 8785 (canonical JSON); OpenTimestamps. Other jurisdictions differ.

A foundation declaration, drafted from a real dossier

A hash does not certify itself: the rules above ask for a qualified person to say what they did and saw. Pick a dossier and this page drafts that declaration from the ledger it just downloaded, after re-checking the whole chain and this dossier's hash in your own browser. What the record does not prove

  
    
    
  • That a decision was right, or that anyone should have acted on it. Every position is simulated; no order is ever placed.

  • That a source said something true, or was not manipulated.
    • 
    
  • Who operated the system at collection time. Integrity after recording is the claim.

  • That a court will admit it, or how much weight it will carry.
