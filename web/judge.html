Bounty "Autonomous Agents": the four things it asks for

1. Agents that interpret market evidence

"Build agents that interpret market evidence…"

  • Each decision freezes one evidence packet: candles, funding, news, and public voices, with the source and time of every item: verdict_desk/evidence.py
  • Two LLM analysts (bull and bear) plus a deterministic indicator engine (RSI, MACD, Bollinger) read that same packet: verdict_desk/agents_v2.py
  • The verdict is deterministic code, not an LLM’s call: the action is the arithmetic of the weighted arguments in verdict_desk/agents_v2.py; a model’s ruling enters only as one argument, capped at 1.0 against an entry band of 1.5, so it can nudge a verdict and never create one, and the same frozen inputs replay to the same verdict (python3 -m verdict_desk replay). A test fails if that cap ever reaches the band.
  • A narrative agent extracts token, stance, conviction and urgency from up to 20 user-selected voices (a file you edit: config/voices.json, whose hash is frozen in every dossier together with the voices that returned nothing that hour; today 5 newsrooms and 8 public Telegram channels) and requires two distinct voices to agree before it reports a convergence: verdict_desk/narrative.py; it also freezes the tokens it rejected and why (one voice only, voices disagreeing, or nobody taking a side)
  • "Trusted voices converge" is measured, not asserted: each voice's calls are scored against the market's move, set against the hit rate luck alone would give, and weighted by the skill that is left, recomputed in your browser from the raw ledger and with no lookahead (every dossier freezes the table it used, and trust-check recomputes it from the dossiers before it): Market voices, verdict_desk/trust.py
  • Your limit, your position: on the same page, tick the voices that agree, set your own risk limit and watch the practice position it produces (the formulas are the Python judge's, compared over hundreds of random inputs; a limit above the 25% ceiling is refused)
  • The scorecard scores every position against what the market did next, with the sample sizes and the caveats printed next to the numbers, the one page that asks whether the decisions were good, not whether the record is intact
  • Every decision can be rendered as a printable audit exhibit: a Certificate of Filing with a VERIFIED seal, and the anchored record also ships as a signed PDF: evidence custody, each claim beside the evidence text it cites, the deciding argument, the recomputed hash, the on-chain anchor, and an explicit statement of what the record does not prove
  • Behaviour you can test instead of trust: the cause-and-effect lab shows which single argument decides a recorded verdict, and replays the policy on inputs you choose, checked against the Python judge on 486 input combinations

2. Record the practice trades they would make

"…and record the practice trades they would make."

  • Every dossier carries entry, size, stop and target, sized from a fixed risk budget; no real order is ever placed
  • It commits instead of hiding behind a HOLD: as of dossier 779, 505 of them carry a sized LONG or SHORT; the other 274 are labelled FLAT (63) or WATCH_REVERSAL (211), each naming the argument or condition that would flip it. The live counts are the Positions card on the dashboard
  • Positions are marked against later candles; the dashboard shows the simulated result of each one: verdict_desk/portfolio.py
  • Open any row in the dashboard to see the trade and the reasoning that produced it

3. Cause and effect

"Strong entries show cause and effect…"

  • Every argument names the evidence item it rests on, and an LLM claim is discarded unless its numbers really appear in the item it cites
  • Each dossier freezes a sensitivity table: what would flip the verdict to LONG or SHORT, and what each single argument is worth
  • Stale evidence is refused instead of used: past a 2-hour window the judge returns BLOCKED_STALE and sizes no position, and the refusal is recorded
  • A declined entry is named, not hidden: WATCH_REVERSAL when one single argument would flip the decision (and the rationale says which), REDUCE_EXPOSURE when both sides are strong and volatility is high; entry decisions and sizing are unchanged from the earlier rule set

4. A repeatable reasoning trail

"…and preserve a repeatable reasoning trail."

  • Dossiers are hash-chained; the dashboard recomputes every SHA-256 in your browser and lets you break the chain yourself with one button
  • python3 -m verdict_desk replay re-derives every verdict from its own frozen evidence, and all of them match
  • python3 -m verdict_desk export --seq N --out pack.json hands one dossier to a third party as a self-verifying file
  • The scale behind the page: 567 tests, standard library only, pin the ledger, the pages and the docs to each other - one of them re-runs scripts/refresh_numbers.py --check, so a figure that no longer matches the chain, a link to a renamed card, or a doc that disagrees with the ledger fails the build instead of shipping

Bounty "Best workflow with CRE": what the workflow does

Chainlink CRE, on Sepolia

  • The workflow fetches the published ledger head over HTTP from two independent publications (the raw repository file and the Pages site), requires them to be identical and every node to agree under DON consensus, reads the BTC/USD Data Feed on Sepolia, and writes both through the CRE forwarder. If either source is down, or they differ, it anchors nothing
  • The receiver only accepts forwarder reports with a strictly increasing sequence number, so an anchored head can never be overwritten
  • The contract is source-verified with an exact match: the explorer serves the code that matches the deployed bytecode: Sourcify record
  • Address 0x50443D51Db71A61E131B147B1d36aF21d48CDdF7 · anchor transactions: #80, the first (block 11964023) and #212, the latest (25 Oct), both SUCCESS; the contract answers isAnchored(212, hash) TRUE
  • The workflow is re-run against the live head, not a fixture: the simulation recorded in cre/evidence/simulate-live-head.txt fetched ledger/head.json under consensus, read the Data Feed at 81,317.07 and encoded record #113, and a test checks that the sequence and hash it names are the ones actually published in the ledger, so the evidence cannot drift from the record
  • Anchoring is a script, not a ceremony: cre/scripts/anchor-existing.sh anchors the current head against the same deployed contract (a new deployment each run would orphan the history). It refuses to run without a key rather than silently doing nothing. The hourly job is wired and reports a skip that says why; until the CRE organisation gets deployment access (needed for an API key) the anchor is sent by hand, and the Witnesses page shows how far the ledger is past the last one
  • And a clock that needs no wallet: every hourly run also stamps the published head with OpenTimestamps, which commits the digest into a Bitcoin block through independent public calendars. Proofs are archived per record (ledger/timestamps/) and a test checks each snapshot equals the published hash: ots verify ledger/timestamps/116.json.ots
  • And it is monitored, not just written once: python3 -m verdict_desk monitor reads the anchored value back from the contract and compares it with the dossier of that same sequence in the published ledger. It says MATCH, or it names the divergence. The hourly job runs it as a guard: a mismatch fails the run.

Beyond the brief: why this record is hard to fake

  • Witnesses: every hour the head of the chain is stamped in Bitcoin. Your browser reads the proof, recomputes the digest, replays the hash steps and asks two independent block explorers for the block's merkle root; it also asks the public Sigstore transparency log, where a GitHub workflow signs the head every hour with no key of ours, and reads the Sepolia anchor, and checks the ledger against every stamped head. A ledger cut short, or rewritten and re-hashed, disagrees with a head that was already stamped.
  • We attack our own verifier and publish the count: python3 -m verdict_desk battery edits a value six ways (down to a Cyrillic look-alike letter), deletes, swaps, duplicates, forges a head, cuts the tail and rewrites history, and runs the project's own verify. Loading the latest result…
  • Evidence for a lawyer: layer by layer, what the record establishes and what it does not (including that our time stamp is not an eIDAS qualified one), and a foundation declaration drafted from a real dossier after re-checking the chain in your browser.

What is real, what is testnet, and what is simulated

  • Real: the market data, the decisions, the hash chain, the browser verification, the replay, the LLM debate with its validator, the AI judge's bounded vote, and the Sepolia anchor.
  • Executed on Sepolia testnet: new practice trades. Each ETHUSDT position is a swap on Uniswap v3's WETH/USDC pool in the same transaction as its receipt, and its stop or target sends the reverse swap; BTC and SOL, which have no testnet market, get an on-chain receipt marked "not executed". The transaction carries a digest of the decision and the dossier records the transaction before it is sealed (how it works). Test tokens only: the testnet pool price is not the market price.
  • Simulated: the money, and every position recorded before the Sepolia desk was switched on. Positions are marked against the market candles frozen in later dossiers, and the scoreboard shows the result before and after a declared taker fee.
  • Who decides: the agent is the whole pipeline: it gathers the evidence, two analysts argue, an AI judge votes on the claims that passed the citation check, code sizes the position inside your risk limit, and the trade is sent. The judge's vote is capped below the entry band on purpose, so no single model output can open a position on its own and every verdict replays exactly.
  • Known limits: one exchange mirror for candles, one rule set, slippage not modelled, and model text that is frozen and re-validated rather than regenerated.

Model routing, measured: which model answers, and why that one

The analysts and the judge run on free models behind a gateway whose code is in the repository (tools/llm-gateway/gate.py). Free models hit rate limits, so each call is routed: the gateway tries the models in the order of a routing policy computed from this ledger by a rule fixed in advance (rank by the Wilson lower bound of the share of each model's claims that passed the citation check, with at least 20 claims; fewer claims means under observation), and moves on only when a model is unavailable or gives an empty answer. A test fails if the policy file is not exactly what the rule gives, so nobody tunes the order by hand. Every new dossier records the route of each call (the models tried, their status, the milliseconds) and the fingerprint of the policy it followed. The bench measures citation precision, not market skill: with this few rulings that would be noise.
