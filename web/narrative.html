The spotlight feature: watch market voices, extract what they mention and how strongly, and report a narrative only when two or more distinct voices agree on the same token and stance. One voice is a claim; two agreeing is a signal. Every token that never converged is kept with the reason why.

The engine, at a glance

Each headline is read by the LLM into a closed vocabulary — token, stance (bullish / bearish / neutral), conviction (low→extreme), urgency — and must carry a literal quote from the headline. A grounding gate refuses an extraction whose quote or token is not in the source. Only validated extractions are counted; the rejected ones are kept too.

Which voices earned trust

"Trusted voices converge" only means something if trust is measured. This table is recomputed here, in your browser, from the raw ledger: nothing below comes from a server. Each accepted bullish or bearish call is scored by the market's move over the next 4 hours, read from the candles frozen in the dossiers. Hit rate alone flatters a voice that only ever says "up" in a rising market, so it is set beside the hit rate luck alone would give for that voice's own bias; skill is what is left, shrunk toward zero on small samples. A voice with fewer than 8 settled calls has no record yet and counts as 1.00. Weights stay inside 0.5 to 1.5.

Try it: your voices, your limit, the position that follows

Pick the voices that agree, choose how much of a simulated $10,000 you are willing to lose, and see the practice position the same rules would open on the latest recorded market for that token. Every number is computed in your browser by the same formulas the Python judge uses, and a test compares the two over hundreds of random inputs. Nothing here is a trade: it is the rule, laid open.

What a convergence does

A convergence is not decoration — it creates the position by a transparent rule: a convergence matching the symbol becomes a deterministic argument on that side, with weight 0.5 × each voice's track-record weight (1.00 until it has a record; the four heaviest voices count) + 0.5 if top conviction is high (rules v5; dossiers recorded under v4 count 0.5 per voice, flat). Two voices are a nudge; four trusted voices are decisive. The same convergence is also a citable evidence item the analysts may cite. Either way, the trail shows exactly which voices said it and the weight each carried.

  • the voices are named, so a convergence can be checked back to its sources
  • the stance is closed-vocabulary, so "bullish" means the same thing every run
  • the quote is literal, so a headline cannot be paraphrased into a stronger signal
