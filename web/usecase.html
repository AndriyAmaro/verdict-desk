A use case: "why did your agent do that, and what did it know?"

This page walks through one real decision of the live ledger the way a lawyer would. It is not legal advice, and whether a record is admitted is for the court; the page shows what the record lets you establish and what it does not.

The situation. A client says the agent should not have opened a short on BTCUSDT, and asks what it knew when it did. Everything the operator can show on its own, such as a log or a database, is something the operator could have edited afterwards. The example below is dossier #212, recorded …, the one anchored on the Ethereum Sepolia contract.

Six steps, no installation

  1. Open the exhibit. The decision exhibit is a printable document generated from one line of the public ledger. Its seal says whether the record still matches its fingerprint, recomputed when the page is built and again by your browser on the verifier.
  2. Read what the agent saw. Section 1 lists every item the agent had (candles, funding, headlines, public voices) with its source and the time it was collected. An item that failed to load is recorded as a gap, not left out silently.
  3. Read why it acted. The bull and bear arguments each cite the evidence item they rest on. The exhibit marks the single argument that decided the verdict and shows what would have flipped it. Claims the validator refused (for example a number the cited evidence does not contain) are listed with the reason.
  4. Take the pack. The exhibit offers an evidence pack, one JSON file with the record, the recomputed hash, the replayed verdict and the links to the records either side. Download the pack, then paste it into the verifier to recompute the hash in your browser, or follow the numbered steps in the file without our software.
  5. Ask three witnesses outside the operator. The witnesses page asks Bitcoin (through two block explorers), the public Sigstore transparency log and the Sepolia contract whether the head of the chain is the one they saw, at a time the operator does not control.
  6. Draft the foundation declaration. The evidence page drafts a declaration from the real dossier, after re-checking the chain in your browser, for a qualified person to review and sign.

What this lets you show, and what it does not

You can showYou cannot show
The record has not been edited since it was written: any change breaks every later hash.That the record was right, or that a source told the truth. The chain proves integrity after recording.
What the agent had in front of it, with the source and time of each item, and which items were missing.Who wrote the record. Authorship at collection time is not what the chain proves.
That the head of the chain existed no later than a Bitcoin block and a Sigstore log entry, neither controlled by the operator.A qualified electronic time stamp under eIDAS. Ours is supporting evidence, and its weight is for the court.
That the verdict follows from the frozen evidence: the replay re-derives it.That the analysts' wording is sound. Their claims are checked for citation, not for merit.
